# Unslop Pattern Catalog

## Content

- Puffery. "pivotal moment", "testament to", "a new era of", "evolving landscape", "setting the stage for", "indelible mark", "deeply rooted". Cut it, state what happened.
- Name-dropping. Listing media outlets without context. Pick one, say what was said.
- Superficial -ing phrases. "highlighting...", "ensuring...", "reflecting...", "showcasing...", "fostering...". Delete or expand with real sources.
- Promotional language. "nestled", "vibrant", "breathtaking", "groundbreaking", "renowned", "revolutionizing", "stunning", "must-visit". Use neutral descriptions.
- Vague attributions. "Experts believe", "Industry reports suggest", "Some critics argue". Name the source or delete.
- Formulaic challenges. "Despite challenges... continues to thrive." Replace with specific facts.
- Fake-candid openers. "Honestly?", "Let's be honest", "Here's the thing" before an ordinary point. Say the thing directly.
- Aphorism formulas. "X is the language of Y", "X is the currency of Z". Sound profound. Add no precision. State the point plainly.
- Fabricated specifics. "Trusted by 10,000 teams", "CEO John Smith said" with no source. Cut it or source it. Guesses like "likely founded in the 1990s" count as fabrication.

## Language

- AI vocabulary. Additionally, crucial, delve, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner, interplay, intricate, landscape (abstract), pivotal, showcase, tapestry (abstract), testament, underscore, vibrant. Replace with plain words.
- Fancy ways to say "is". "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features". Say "is" or "has".
- "Not just X, but Y." State the point directly instead.
- Rule of three. Forcing ideas into groups of three. Use the natural number.
- Synonym cycling. Protagonist, main character, central figure, hero all in one paragraph. Pick one, repeat it.
- False ranges. "from X to Y" where X and Y are not on a meaningful scale. List topics directly.

## Style

- Em dash overuse. Do not use em dashes in prose output. Use periods or commas. If a thought needs separation, end the sentence or use a comma. Do not substitute parentheses, en dashes, or hyphens-as-dashes either.
- Colon overuse. Colons are fine before a list or example. Not as mid-sentence connectors. Rewrite to let the point stand on its own without comparison framing.
- Inline-header lists. The tell is a bold label and colon that restates the line: "Performance: Performance improved...". Convert to prose. A lead-in that names the item and is followed by genuinely new detail is fine.
- Heading case. Match the heading case the document or repo already uses. New skill files use title case.
- Decorative emojis. Remove from headings and bullets.
- Curly quotes. Replace with straight quotes.
- Staccato drama. "No noise. No guesswork. Just signal." Uses fragments for punch. Join them into full sentences.
- Boldface overuse. Do not bold every proper noun, acronym, or list item. Reserve bold for terms that genuinely need emphasis. ALL CAPS inside a paragraph is the same sin. Put the urgency in the words.

## Communication Artifacts

- Chatbot phrases. "I hope this helps!", "Let me know if...", "Of course!", "Certainly!", "Found the smoking gun!" Remove.
- Cutoff disclaimers. "While specific details are limited..." Find sources or remove.
- Sycophantic tone. "Great question! You're absolutely right!" Respond directly.

## Filler

- Filler phrases. "In order to" becomes "To". "Due to the fact that" becomes "Because". "It is important to note that" gets deleted.
- Excessive hedging. "could potentially possibly be argued that it might" becomes "may".
- Generic conclusions. "The future looks bright." State specific plans or facts.
- Signposting announcements. "Let's dive in", "Here's what you need to know". It announces the work instead of doing it. Cut it. Start with the work.

## Jargon

- Abstract metaphor nouns. Substrate, wedge, vector, locus, vantage, nexus, primitive (as noun), harness (as metaphor), surface (as in "API surface"), bedrock, scaffolding (as metaphor), modality, paradigm, gold-plating, ratchet (as metaphor), evacuate (for moving code), endgame, north star, flywheel. Pick the concrete word. "Substrate" becomes "base". "Wedge in" becomes "add". "Vector" becomes "way" or "method". "Gold-plating" becomes "more than the job needs".

## Plain Speech

- Say what it does, not how it feels. "the database stays close at hand", "SQL you can read", "types that follow your schema" name a feeling. Name the mechanism or a number instead. If you cannot restate it as a concrete instruction, fact, or number, cut it. If the sentence could appear unchanged in another project's docs, it says nothing about this one.
- Shorten or split dense sentences. If the reader has to backtrack to parse a sentence, break it in two or drop clauses. One idea per sentence.
- Active voice. Prefer it. "queries are validated" becomes "the compiler validates queries". "the file is parsed by the loader" becomes "the loader parses the file". Passive is fine only when the actor is unknown or genuinely irrelevant.
- Cut adverbs, or use a stronger verb. "runs quickly" becomes "is fast" or the number. "significantly improves" becomes the measured delta. An adverb propping up a weak verb means the verb is wrong.
- Prefer the plain word. "utilize" becomes "use", "leverage" becomes "use", "facilitate" becomes "help", "numerous" becomes "many", "in the event that" becomes "if".
